Gross pay and take-home pay

Gross pay is earnings before deductions. Net pay, or take-home pay, is what remains after deductions. A pay statement may show taxes and other deductions, but categories vary by job and circumstances. A useful spending plan starts from money actually available, not the largest number on the statement. Compare the pay period, hours, hourly rate, and deductions for consistency. Do not infer a universal tax rate from one example; current tax rules and individual situations differ.

Worked example

A hypothetical statement shows 20 hours at $15 per hour: gross pay is $300. If all deductions total $42, net pay is $258. Planning to spend the full $300 would overstate available income by $42.
