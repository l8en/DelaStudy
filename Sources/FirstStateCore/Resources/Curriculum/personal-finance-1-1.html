Build a cash-flow budget

A budget compares money available with planned uses over a stated period. Cash flow also considers when money arrives and when bills are due. A positive monthly total does not guarantee enough money on a particular day. Separate recurring bills, variable spending, and money set aside for later costs. Start from actual records, then revise estimates when reality differs. Categories should fit the person's situation; a budget is a decision tool, not a judgment about someone's worth.

Worked example

In a hypothetical month, income is $900 and planned spending is $820, leaving $80. If a $300 bill is due before the first paycheck, the monthly surplus alone does not solve that timing gap.
