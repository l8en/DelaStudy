Reports and scores are different

A credit report records information about credit accounts and related activity. A credit score is a number produced by a scoring model using relevant information. A person can have different scores because models, underlying reports, and calculation dates differ. Neither a score nor a report is a complete measure of someone's financial life or personal value. When reviewing a report, check identity details and account information for accuracy rather than assuming every unfamiliar item is correct.

Worked example

Two hypothetical lenders use different scoring models on different dates. Their reported scores need not match, even if the student has not opened another account. The useful next step is to understand the source and review the underlying information.
