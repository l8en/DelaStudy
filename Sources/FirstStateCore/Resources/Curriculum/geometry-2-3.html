Isosceles triangles and midsegments

An isosceles triangle has at least two congruent sides, and the angles opposite those sides are congruent. A triangle midsegment joins the midpoints of two sides. It is parallel to the third side and half its length. These statements refer to corresponding sides and angles; label the diagram so the equal sides are not confused with the base.

Worked example

An isosceles triangle has a vertex angle of 44°. Each base angle measures (180 − 44)/2 = 68°. A midsegment parallel to a 14 cm base measures 7 cm.
