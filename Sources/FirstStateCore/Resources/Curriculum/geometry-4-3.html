Distance, midpoint, and circles

The distance formula comes from the Pythagorean theorem: distance² = (x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)². The midpoint averages each coordinate. A circle centered at (h,k) with radius r has equation (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². The signs inside the parentheses are opposite the center's coordinates.

Worked example

The midpoint of (−2,1) and (4,9) is (1,5), and their distance is √(6² + 8²) = 10. A circle centered at (1,5) with radius 5 has equation (x − 1)² + (y − 5)² = 25.
