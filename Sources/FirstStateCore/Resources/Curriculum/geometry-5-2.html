Volumes and surface areas

Volume measures three-dimensional space in cubic units. A prism has volume Bh, where B is base area and h is perpendicular height. A cylinder follows the same rule with circular base area πr². A pyramid or cone with the same base area and height has one third the corresponding prism or cylinder volume. Surface area instead measures the exposed two-dimensional boundary.

Worked example

A cylinder of radius 3 and height 10 has volume 90π cubic units. Its total surface area is 2πr² + 2πrh = 18π + 60π = 78π square units.
